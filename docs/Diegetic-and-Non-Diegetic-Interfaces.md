Users can interact with XR systems through different types of interfaces, which are commonly divided into diegetic and non-diegetic elements. [Diegetic Interfaces](Diegetic-Interfaces.md) are embedded directly within the virtual environment and exist as part of the world itself, such as a backpack used as an inventory or a tool like a screwdriver to unscrew screws. Non-diegetic interfaces are typically 2D elements (i.e. [Billboard Interfaces](Graphical-User-Interface.md)) that are not part of the virtual world but are presented to support interaction and understanding. Examples include heads-up displays (HUDs), floating panels, or markers that convey information such as status, progress, or instructions.

You can read more about [Diegetic Interfaces](Diegetic-Interfaces.md) and [Billboard Interfaces](Graphical-User-Interface.md) in their respective sections.

When to use diegetic interfaces can depend on several factors, including the type of application, its duration and intensity, the user’s experience level, and the nature of the interaction itself.
