Many XR applications involve an additional person, such as a supervisor or trainer, who is next to the user and guides them through the application. These instructors often guide users through tasks, provide explanations, or adjust interactions in real time. Depending on the complexity of the application and the user’s familiarity with XR, this external support can play a crucial role in helping users navigate the experience efficiently and confidently. To be effective, instructors need a well-designed set of tools that allows them to guide the user, intervene when necessary, and adapt the environment to the user’s needs without disrupting the experience.

* **Favour Consistency:** While some applications vary in type and use case, they often have similar interaction needs for instructors. When tools or controls behave differently despite serving the same purpose, it increases the likelihood of confusion and mistakes, especially for instructors who work across multiple applications. A consistent instructor toolkit across applications improves learnability, reduces mental effort, and makes it easier for instructors to transfer skills between experiences.
* **Provide Clear and Accessible Instructions:** Not all instructor features are immediately obvious. Instructors should have a clear overview of available tools and understand how and when to use them. Without this, useful interactions may be overlooked or misused. Just as users benefit from ongoing access to onboarding materials (see [Onboarding](Onboarding.md)), instructors should also have continuous access to instructions and reference materials for their tools. \[[12, 53](References.md)\]
* **Keep Instructor Interactions Simple:** The technical and XR experience can vary depending on the instructors. Simple, direct interactions help reduce frustration and allow instructors to focus on supporting the user rather than managing complex controls. Complex feature activations, such as confusing hotkey combinations, should be avoided to make the instructor interface more approachable and reliable. \[[54](References.md)\]
* **Create Common Visual Ground:** Instructors should have access to at least a mirrored view of what the user is seeing. Guiding someone through an XR application without knowing their exact perspective makes support more difficult and can quickly become confusing for both parties. Since mirrored views can still have limitations, such as a reduced field of view or unstable camera movement, it is beneficial to allow instructors to enter the virtual environment themselves or provide them with multiple viewpoints of the user. This shared visual context helps instructors give clearer, more precise guidance and improves overall communication. \[[12](References.md)\]
* **Common Useful Features:** While instructor needs vary by application, certain tools are broadly useful across many XR experiences. For example, space-related applications often benefit from features that allow instructors to teleport the user, adjust lighting conditions (such as the sun’s position), or highlight objects using lasers or outlines. Other functions that could be beneficial for multiple application are the options to reset bigger action, control the rotation or movement of the user (e.g. stop uncontrolled rotation to reduce motion sickness), navigate to the user if the instructor’s view is independent of the user’s view, activate controller diagram or virtual controller if the user seems confused, and have the options to switch perspective if the instructor has access to multiple camera view.
